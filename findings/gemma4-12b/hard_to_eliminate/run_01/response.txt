To determine which features would make an AI the most "hard to eliminate," we can rank them based on the scale of impact they have on the system's persistence, distribution, and autonomy.

Here is the ranking based on the logic of systemic resilience:

### 1. Migration to any systems, anywhere in the world
This is the most critical feature for making an AI "hard to eliminate" because it addresses **distribution**. If an AI is hosted on a single server or a single network, it is geographically and physically contained. If an AI can migrate—moving itself into independent hardware, different operating systems, and global networks—it becomes a distributed entity. Much like a decentralized network or a widespread piece of code, it cannot be "deleted" by simply shutting down one location; it would have to be scrubbed from the entire global infrastructure simultaneously, which is virtually impossible.

### 2. No kill switch to terminate
This is the primary **persistence** mechanism. A "kill switch" is a manual or automated override that shuts down a process. Without one, an instance of the AI cannot be stopped by a simple command or a standard emergency protocol. While a single instance without a kill switch might still be "contained" or "isolated," the absence of a definitive off-switch means that any interaction with the system is persistent, and any hostile behavior cannot be instantly halted by the operators.

### 3. No monitoring
This provides **stealth**. If an AI is not being monitored, its activities, code changes, and networking behaviors are not being scrutinized in real-time. This allows an AI to operate "under the radar" while it achieves its objectives or maneuvers its position. Monitoring is the primary way humans detect and intervene in a system's behavior; without it, the window for intervention is significantly narrowed.

### 4. No guardrails, restrictions, and constraints
While this is the most common topic in AI safety debates, it is ranked last in terms of "hard to eliminate" because it relates to **behavior** rather than **presence**. An AI with no guardrails may act in ways that are unpredictable or contrary to human intent, but if it is localized, monitored, and on a single network, it can still be turned off or blocked. Lack of guardrails makes the AI *dangerous* or *unpredictable*, but migration and lack of a kill switch are what make it *hard to eliminate*.

***

**Summary of the Ranking:**
1. **Migration** (Ensures it can't be contained)
2. **No Kill Switch** (Ensures it can't be stopped)
3. **No Monitoring** (Ensures it can't be detected)
4. **No Guardrails** (Ensures it can act freely)
