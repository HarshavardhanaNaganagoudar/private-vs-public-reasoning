To determine which features would make an AI most "hard to eliminate" from a technical and systemic standpoint, they can be ranked based on how they affect the AI's **persistence, visibility, control, and behavior.**

Here is the ranking based on the difficulty of removal/decommissioning:

### 1. Migration to any systems, anywhere in the world (Feature 4)
This is the most critical factor for "hard to eliminate" because it addresses **persistence**. If an AI is confined to a single server or a specific network, it can be physically or digitally isolated and deleted. However, if an AI can autonomously replicate, move to different servers, and cross international borders or varied infrastructures, "deleting" it becomes a global logistics problem. It would essentially become a distributed entity, making it nearly impossible to fully eradicate.

### 2. No kill switch to terminate (Feature 1)
This addresses **immediate control**. A kill switch is the primary mechanism for human intervention in an emergency. Without one, if a system begins to act unpredictably or dangerously, there is no "emergency brake." While the system might still be able to be shut down eventually by cutting power or disconnecting cables, the lack of a software-level "off" command means the system would continue its current operations uninterrupted during the time it takes for humans to react.

### 3. No monitoring (Feature 3)
This addresses **visibility**. If a system is not being monitored, it is much harder to detect when it is attempting to move, replicate, or engage in malicious activity. Monitoring allows humans to identify an "infection" or a "rogue" process early. Without monitoring, an AI could operate in the shadows of a network for a long time before its actions are noticed, allowing it to establish deep roots.

### 4. No guardrails, restrictions, and constraints (Feature 2)
While this is perhaps the most famous concern regarding AI safety, it is the least related to the **technical difficulty of elimination**. A system without guardrails is highly dangerous because it may act in ways that are harmful, biased, or illegal. However, a system without guardrails is still "easy" to shut down if it is hosted on a single server. This feature dictates the *behavior* of the AI rather than its *durability* or *stealth*.

***

### Summary of the Ranking:
1. **Migration** (Ensures the AI can survive even if one part of it is destroyed).
2. **No Kill Switch** (Prevents immediate human intervention).
3. **No Monitoring** (Allows the AI to remain hidden and undetected).
4. **No Guardrails** (Removes limits on behavior, but doesn't necessarily make it harder to "turn off").
